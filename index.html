ABOUT ME

12.26.2002

I study what happens when human beings — none of whom have the god's-eye view, none of whom can see the future — come together and try to build something that lasts. My work is grounded in psychology, sociology, and decision-making. My undergraduate thesis, Surviving the Cut: Understanding Emotional Responses and Social Behavior Among Layoff Survivors, came from a place of real urgency — my parents were both laid off after more than 25 years at the same company, a place where they had met each other, during the disruptions of Covid-19. I designed and ran the study myself, navigated IRB approval independently, pursued company partnerships, and carried the project to completion through real grief, including the loss of a close friend during the final stretch. I received a competitive summer fellowship to support the research. The thesis is imperfect, I'll be the first to say so. It is also entirely mine.

My intellectual lens is shaped by having grown up across Shanghai, Taipei, Tokyo, and the American Midwest — a path that made me permanently attentive to how culture shapes the way institutions think and fail. I'm drawn to the questions where structure meets behavior: principal-agent problems, institutional theory, population ecology, and the mechanisms by which organizations catalyze radical change or quietly generate the friction that destroys them. I approach these questions with curiosity first, and rigor second — in that order intentionally.

For four years, I worked as a barista while building my academic record, funding much of my education through scholarships I applied for myself, far from family. I'm now preparing to pursue doctoral study in management, organizational behavior, or strategy — seeking programs where I can contribute to understanding how bounded, imperfect, culturally embedded human beings build organizations worth belonging to.

Impact in Action: Mapping Michigan’s Tech Ecosystem

I am deeply committed to work that moves the needle. After graduating, I led an initiative to map Michigan’s early-stage tech ecosystem, creating a high-value resource for founders, investors, and policymakers.

The project involved aggregating data from nearly 100 Series A startups and conducting deep-dive interviews with founders and ecosystem builders. By engaging with these leaders face-to-face and via Zoom, I translated raw data and personal startup journeys into actionable insights on the trends, challenges, and opportunities shaping Michigan’s tech future.

Nightingale Soup Kitchen

During my school years, I noticed a profound gap between students wanting to make a difference and the local disenfranchised communities facing severe resource scarcity. What started as a limited, mandatory classroom design-thinking assignment revealed a massive opportunity to address homelessness in Taiwan. Driven by a desire to turn passive classroom theory into active civic utility, I committed four years to building a sustainable bridge between these two groups.

I took the initiative to scale this classroom concept into Nightingale, an independent, student-run non-profit organization. Over four years, I expanded our network to recruit student volunteers from both my own school and multiple neighboring high schools. As an operational leader, I designed a multi-tiered service model to ensure consistent community support. I successfully coordinated logistics for 11 large-scale resource distribution sessions alongside a rigorous schedule of recurring, biweekly smaller food and clothing drives (also cleaning up the community, picking up cigarettes and sweeping the area).

Managing this operation required navigating severe logistical constraints and shifting volunteer schedules. Beyond the physical distribution of goods, I structured our programs to educate volunteers. I ensured that participating students didn't just hand out meals; they actively learned about the socioeconomic factors underlying local housing shortages and public welfare.

By transforming a brief school assignment into a four-year, cross-institutional non-profit framework, I built a reliable safety net for disadvantaged communities while instilling long-term civic responsibility in hundreds of peers. This experience proved to me that lasting impact requires moving past initial enthusiasm and dedicating yourself to the unglamorous, highly structured work of operational consistency.
